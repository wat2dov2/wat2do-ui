The proposed offer

Campus Community Partnership

Make campus life easier to find, without giving the union another platform to maintain.

wat2do contributes

  • A maintained school discovery page, with an agreed set of clubs and public event sources.
  • Launch copy and social graphics, plus a direct route for corrections.
  • A monthly one-page report: discovery trends, broad search themes and practical programming ideas.
  • A preview before promotion, a named contact, and a written handoff for next year’s student leaders.

The union contributes

  • One liaison and an introduction to the clubs or student-life team.
  • An agreed resource-page link, one newsletter mention and two social placements during the pilot, subject to its policies.
  • Existing public event / club sources and occasional corrections.
  • A short end-of-pilot review to decide what is useful.

No fees, commissions, revenue share, exclusivity or paid conversion. Continue free by mutual agreement after the pilot. The purpose is student connection and a useful campus resource.

This is a proposal to offer, not an existing service-level agreement. Agree launch scope and maintenance capacity before promising dates. The school record in the database alone does not prove that its listings are complete.

Make it easy to say yes

Adaptation of Alex Hormozi’s value equation in $100M Offers: increase the desired outcome and confidence in delivery; reduce the wait and the effort. This is a mission-led exchange, not a pricing exercise.

Show, then ask

Send a relevant campus preview and an example report layout before asking for promotion. Use clearly labelled mock data until real figures are available.

Own the work

Prepare listings, copy, graphics and reports. Ask the union to approve and distribute, not to rebuild its calendar or enter every event twice.

Remove the commitment risk

Start with a short pilot. If the page is not ready, pause promotion. If it is not useful, either side can stop. Promise deliverables, not attendance or engagement growth.

Framework source: Acquisition.com offer training and value checklist. The partnership design here is our application of the framework.

What the analytics report should actually contain

Start with a manually prepared report. A school-facing analytics dashboard was not verified. Existing discovery logging supports some report ingredients; it does not establish that every school has sufficient data.

InsightUseful questionReadiness / honest interpretation
Discovery activityWhich school surfaces are students using to look for things?Code records school, surface, timestamp, query and filters. Count logged discovery interactions by week and surface. These are not unique users; empty queries and filter changes can also generate records.
Broad search themesAre students looking for volunteering, arts, sports or other communities?Aggregate and manually redact queries into themes. Suppress sparse or sensitive themes. Define a minimum reporting threshold before launch; do not export raw searches, page URLs or individual histories.
Engagement and repeat useDo visitors return, explore listings or click through?Desired metric. Validate school-scoped event instrumentation, consent and metric definitions first. GA configuration alone is not proof these events exist. Do not promise this report field until verified.
Promotion performanceWhich union placements bring useful visits?Proposed tagged links and agreed attribution window. Requires setup and validation. Report observed visits/clicks, not causal claims about belonging or attendance.
Unmet demandWhat might the union or clubs offer next?Use repeated broad search themes as prompts for discussion. Zero-result rates require search-result instrumentation not confirmed here. Search volume is interest, not a representative student survey.

Local evidence reviewed: backend/services/discovery_query_service.py, frontend/src/shared/hooks/useDiscoveryQueryTracking.ts, and the app’s analytics configuration. No student-level data was accessed for this guide.

Suggested report layout: date range and coverage → discovery activity → broad interests → promotion observations where supported → three practical next actions. State missing data and changes in measurement.

How to use this directory

Start with the recommended contact. Student-life, clubs and programming portfolios usually fit this offer better than a board-wide email. President and staff routes provide backup.

Check the linked roster before sending, personalize the editable draft, and follow up once after about a week. Ask for the right person when the role is uncertain.

Coverage is complete by school, not by every seat. This compiles executive leaders, available directors and selected relevant staff. Each card identifies roster and committee gaps. Most routes focus on undergraduate representation; separate graduate unions and constituent councils are not exhaustively mapped.

Sources are public official directories and governance pages. A live page without a stated term is labelled accordingly. No email address has been inferred from a naming pattern.

Drafts are editable in this file. Use Save edited copy to keep changes and notes in a new local HTML file; reloading without saving loses edits. Nothing is sent or uploaded. Source links need internet; the directory itself works offline.
